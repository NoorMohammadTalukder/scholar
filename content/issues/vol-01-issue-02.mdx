Measurement is the quiet foundation of every empirical discipline, and it is rarely examined on its own terms. This issue brings together three articles that make measurement itself the object of study.

Leila Haddad and colleagues report on a network of low-cost water sensors deployed across Beirut, and on what the community learned from the data. Priya Raghunathan takes a hard look at the benchmarks used to rank language models. Ingrid Bauer and Amara Okafor show how causal methods can extract more from air-quality monitors than correlation ever could.
