## Introduction

Low-emission zones are among the most common urban environmental policies. Their evaluation is complicated by weather, seasonality, and the fact that cities adopting them differ from those that do not.

## Data and method

We assembled hourly NO2 and PM2.5 readings from 212 monitors in 14 treated cities and 61 control cities between 2015 and 2024. For each treated city we constructed a synthetic control from a weighted combination of untreated cities matched on pre-intervention pollution trajectories, meteorology, and population.

## Results

Post-intervention NO2 fell relative to synthetic controls in all fourteen cities, with effects ranging from 8% to 19%. Placebo tests on untreated cities produced effects of this size in fewer than 3% of permutations.

<Figure caption="Figure 1. Treated versus synthetic NO2 for Milan. The vertical line marks introduction of Area B.">
Observed and synthetic series track closely before the intervention and diverge afterward.
</Figure>

We found no effect on PM2.5 in any city, consistent with its larger regional and non-traffic sources.

## Discussion

Traffic restrictions work for the pollutant most directly tied to vehicle exhaust and do little for particulates. Cities targeting PM2.5 need different tools.
