## Introduction

Roughly one billion people receive piped water for only part of each day. Managing such systems requires knowing when water arrives at each tap, information that utilities rarely possess. We asked whether residents could collect it themselves.

## Sensor design

Each unit combines a pressure transducer, a flow switch, an ESP32 microcontroller, and a LoRaWAN radio. Bill of materials was $18.40 at 200 units.

## Deployment and adoption

We recruited households through neighbourhood committees. Adoption followed a classic diffusion curve, but with a twist: uptake accelerated sharply once a public dashboard went live, suggesting that visible collective benefit mattered more than individual convenience.

## Economic analysis

Households with sensors reduced spending on trucked water by a median of 23% over six months, chiefly by timing storage tank fills to the supply schedule the data revealed.

## Discussion

The engineering was the easy part. The durable outcome was institutional: three neighbourhood committees now negotiate pumping schedules with the utility using their own data.
