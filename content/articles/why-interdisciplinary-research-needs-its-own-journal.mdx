## The problem with lanes

Every discipline maintains a set of conventions about what counts as a contribution. These conventions are useful: they let reviewers judge work quickly and let readers trust what they find. But they also create blind spots. A study that imports a method from another field will often fail the home field's conventions in ways that have nothing to do with its quality.

## How we review

Every submission to *Scholar* is read by at least two referees from different disciplines. We ask each referee to answer a simple question: *does this work change what someone in your field could do or believe?* We do not ask whether it follows your field's conventions.

<Callout title="Our commitment">
We publish reviews alongside articles when authors and referees consent, and we never charge authors to publish.
</Callout>

## What we ask of authors

Write for a reader who is expert in one of the fields you draw on and a novice in the others. Define terms. Explain why a method was chosen, not only how it was applied. Share your data and code.
