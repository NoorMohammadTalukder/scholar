## Introduction

When we say a model "scores 87% on a benchmark," we are making a measurement claim. Psychometrics has spent a century developing tools to examine such claims. This article applies them.

## Method

We fit two-parameter logistic item response models to per-item results from 48 models across twelve benchmarks.

<Equation>
P(correct<sub>ij</sub>) = 1 / (1 + exp(−a<sub>i</sub>(θ<sub>j</sub> − b<sub>i</sub>)))
</Equation>

where θ is the latent ability of model *j*, and *a* and *b* are the discrimination and difficulty of item *i*.

## Findings

- Between 30% and 60% of items in each benchmark had discrimination parameters indistinguishable from zero.
- Removing those items changed the rank order of top-ten models in nine of twelve benchmarks.
- Test information curves show most benchmarks are informative only in a narrow ability band that current frontier models have already passed.

<Callout title="Implication">
A one-point difference between two models on most benchmarks is within measurement error. Leaderboards should report confidence intervals derived from item-level uncertainty.
</Callout>

## Recommendations

We release a toolkit for computing item statistics and propose that benchmark maintainers publish discrimination and difficulty estimates alongside aggregate scores.
